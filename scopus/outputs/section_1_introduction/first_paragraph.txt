Food analytics is an interdisciplinary field that employs advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence, big data, machine learning, computer vision, and spectroscopy—including near-infrared (NIR) and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy—alongside scientific methods from molecular biology and chemistry to systematically investigate, identify, and quantify components within food systems [UT 20][UT 53][UT 114][UT 193][UT 196][UT 398][UT 428][UT 431]. Integrating data-driven analytic tools with knowledge of complex molecular signalling pathways enables methodological innovation in areas such as metabolomics, gastrointestinal studies, and automated dietary assessments, including calorie counting, while supporting improved diagnostic precision for food quality, authenticity, safety, and nutritional value [UT 193][UT 196][UT 428]. These approaches enhance the monitoring of food composition and contaminants, facilitate fraud detection, support regulatory compliance, and drive public health initiatives through the development of derived databases and analytic capabilities tailored to food quality control and consumer transparency [UT 20][UT 53][UT 398][UT 428]. The incorporation of such sophisticated analytical strategies underlines food analytics’ increasing importance in delivering accurate, efficient, and health-focused solutions throughout the food supply chain [UT 20][UT 196][UT 428].
