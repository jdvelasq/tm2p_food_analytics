This thematic cluster defines the integration of machine learning, deep learning, and artificial intelligence within food analytics, encompassing predictive, explainable, and optimization models tailored to the multidimensional realities of contemporary food systems. A clear trend emerges as the field moves away from traditional, labor-intensive analytics to adopt scalable, data-driven, and increasingly explainable models that leverage supervised and unsupervised approaches, ensemble and gradient architectures, attention mechanisms, SHAP values, and multimodal, high-dimensional data sources spanning images, sensory readings, genomic profiles, and consumer behavior indices [UT 1189, UT 1171, UT 987, UT 1234, UT 256, UT 974, UT 1124, UT 1233; UT 1048, UT 1056, UT 810, UT 1045, UT 358, UT 342]. This methodological evolution facilitates breakthroughs in food safety, quality control, fraud detection, supply chain optimization, forecasting, consumer preference analysis, and personalized recommendations [UT 25, UT 920, UT 253, UT 283, UT 1904, UT 825]. Critical challenges persist, notably the scarcity of high-quality, regionally diverse, and well-annotated datasets; technical barriers to integrating heterogeneous data streams; limited interpretability of deep and ensemble models; and infrastructural, computational, and ethical constraints inhibiting both scalability and equity [UT 1189, UT 974, UT 884, UT 943, UT 940, UT 881, UT 2052, UT 756, UT 1027, UT 881, UT 1233, UT 966]. To confront these, research opportunities abound: advancing explainable AI through Shapley values and attention, standardizing and democratizing multimodal datasets, deploying adaptive and federated frameworks for real-time analytics, and ensuring model robustness in extreme data and dynamic environments [UT 893, UT 1189, UT 327, UT 1009, UT 974, UT 1167, UT 2052, UT 248, UT 44, UT 756, UT 966, UT 941, UT 1233]. The future trajectory calls for unified protocols in data curation and benchmarking, cross-disciplinary partnerships, ethical and privacy-centric governance, and participatory upskilling to bridge the gap between algorithmic innovation and end-user value. Practically, the cluster recommends standardizing the end-to-end data lifecycle, fostering open, interoperable systems, embedding explainable, computationally efficient models, and aligning with global sustainability benchmarks to ensure robust, scalable impact in food quality, safety, traceability, and consumer empowerment [UT 224, UT 943, UT 883, UT 921, UT 1155, UT 1904, UT 253, UT 881, UT 342, UT 993, UT 1024].
