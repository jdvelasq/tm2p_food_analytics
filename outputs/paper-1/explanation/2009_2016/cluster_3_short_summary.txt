This thematic cluster in food analytics is defined by the convergence of advanced analytical chemistry, computational intelligence, and semantic informatics to predict, assess, and manage the properties, authenticity, and health impacts of food and beverages, drawing on compositional, temperature, concentration, and molecular data to advance food quality, safety, and provenance [UT 2040][UT 2077][UT 421][UT 664][UT 398][UT 425]. Recent trends reveal an accelerated diversification toward integrated, high-throughput approaches, including chemometrics, soft computing (e.g., neural and neuro-fuzzy models), and semantic frameworks that enable nuanced prediction of bioactive, chemical, and microbial profiles for applications ranging from compositional analytics to public health interventions [UT 1735][UT 1796][UT 441][UT 425][UT 1827][UT 2097][UT 452]. The proliferation of large-scale, semantic web-enabled food, beverage, and chemical databases is foundational for structuring and unifying knowledge, supporting applications such as personalized nutrition, supply-chain traceability, and dietary risk assessment [UT 421][UT 435][UT 446][UT 1554][UT 1654]. Despite this momentum, persistent challenges arise from food matrix complexity, high data dimensionality, methodological fragmentation, variable data quality, and the limited granularity and interoperability of bioactive molecule and microorganism databases [UT 2040][UT 1735][UT 441][UT 452][UT 425][UT 2094][UT 2062][UT 664][UT 857][UT 446][UT 1554][UT 1654][UT 855][UT 864]. Research opportunities lie in advancing interpretable, transparent predictive models; refining semantic enrichment and ontological interoperability; and harmonizing big data analytics, non-destructive measurement, and exhaustive chemical informatics for smarter provenance, safety, and personalized health predictions [UT 1850][UT 2097][UT 439][UT 664][UT 421][UT 1554][UT 398][UT 2061][UT 2026]. To realize the full practical value of food analytics, stakeholders should prioritize the development of modular, extensible semantic frameworks and robust protocols; expand open, searchable molecule and informatic databases; and make predictive outputs actionable for both experts and the broader agri-food community through user-focused interfaces and collaborative institutional feedback, thereby ensuring trust, reproducibility, and sustained public health impact [UT 857][UT 425][UT 855][UT 1554][UT 2062][UT 1791][UT 2026][UT 452].
