This thematic cluster is defined by the intersection of computational intelligence and domain-specific knowledge, focusing on the integration of intelligent, fuzzy, and semantic methodologies to address foundational challenges in food, nutrition, healthy eating, and disease analytics. Recent trends show a clear shift toward advanced, personalized interventions enabled by knowledge-rich systems—such as ontologies, fuzzy frameworks, and machine learning—that leverage chemical, nutritional, and behavioral data to support more accurate dietary assessment, personalized health recommendations, and nuanced disease management [UT 1787, UT 1739, UT 1619, UT 841, UT 664, UT 435, UT 2026, UT 1993, UT 1992, UT 859, UT 866, UT 862]. The literature foregrounds the importance of unifying structured ontological models with intelligent reasoning and fuzzy approaches (notably type-2 fuzzy logic) to handle uncertainty, variability, and cultural/contextual diversity in eating and nutrition behaviors [UT 8654, UT 871, UT 863, UT 857, UT 879, UT 862, UT 866]. However, ongoing challenges persist in effectively representing the complexity of food habits and health profiles—especially the implicit, fuzzy, and culturally contingent facets—while balancing the need for robust automation, interoperability, and privacy across heterogeneous chemical and health datasets [UT 866, UT 857, UT 861, UT 650, UT 841, UT 867, UT 854, UT 846, UT 406, UT 1654, UT 439, UT 877, UT 876]. Major methodological and implementation barriers include semantic heterogeneity, incomplete or noisy data, intensive domain expert input for ontology and fuzzy system development, and limited empirical evidence of long-term user adoption and health impact [UT 861, UT 650, UT 737, UT 821, UT 863, UT 741, UT 851, UT 864]. Future research must prioritize more expressive, adaptive, and automated knowledge models that capture evolving nutritional trends and lifestyles, as well as hybrid, sensor-linked fuzzy architectures capable of dynamic, context-aware recommendations [UT 871, UT 857, UT 862, UT 850, UT 664, UT 874]. Further opportunities involve advancing user-centered validation frameworks, promoting open standards, and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration to bridge food production, behavioral analytics, public health, and regulatory requirements [UT 435, UT 841, UT 446, UT 406, UT 439]. Practically, the cluster’s impact will be maximized by integrating standardization for interoperability, user-interpretable intelligent interfaces, and dynamic feedback from both end-users and medical experts, ensuring both trust and actionable guidance [UT 2026, UT 1993, UT 764, UT 823, UT 664, UT 853]. Embedding privacy safeguards and interface transparency, while supporting the continual enrichment of knowledge bases through automated extraction and cross-sector collaboration, forms the foundation for robust, adaptive systems that enhance nutrition, health, and food knowledge management and drive tangible improvements in disease prevention and healthy eating outcomes [UT 406, UT 439, UT 1730, UT 1985, UT 523, UT 877, UT 1992, UT 860].
