The field is characterized by a marked shift from generic, popularity-based recommendations to highly tailored, data-driven models that employ a combination of collaborative filtering, content-based filtering, and hybrid approaches—often underpinned by deep learning and knowledge graphs—to finely model user tastes, ingredient preferences, and health contexts [UT 470][UT 301][UT 803][UT 711][UT 1493][UT 701][UT 486][UT 798][UT 109]. Trends highlight the growing importance of multimodal data integration (textual, visual, sensor-based), real-time personalization, and explainability, which collectively enable more adaptive, explainable, and actionable food suggestions [UT 374][UT 1473][UT 547][UT 1493][UT 467][UT 2003][UT 1466][UT 243]. Nevertheless, challenges persist, including harmonizing taste with health and cultural context, overcoming data sparsity and bias, ensuring interpretability, and managing deployment issues such as privacy, cost of data annotation, and stakeholder acceptance [UT 470][UT 544][UT 683][UT 764][UT 1988][UT 697][UT 1955][UT 2039][UT 1949][UT 831][UT 515][UT 519][UT 243][UT 701].
