This thematic cluster is defined by the integration of artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, and advanced chemometrics within digital transformation frameworks (e.g., Industry 4.0) to drive objective, automated, and scalable food analytics across the agri-food system, including fruits, vegetables, meat, animal-derived, and packaged foods [UT 1924; UT 1473; UT 594; UT 1366; UT 2074]. As the field evolves from traditional, labor-intensive practices to sophisticated, data-centric methods, it leverages IoT-enabled smart sensors, spectroscopy (including infrared and hyperspectral approaches), and network-based analytic models to address a wide spectrum of detection, classification, and quality assurance needs, such as origin authentication, pathogen detection, compositional profiling, and real-time monitoring throughout the food supply chain [UT 1467; UT 1885; UT 1392; UT 2115; UT 1304; UT 1264]. Current trends highlight increasing computational sophistication—through hybrid neural architectures and advanced data fusion—alongside growing global research momentum, particularly in China and the US, and the proliferation of interdisciplinary technologies encompassing robotics, blockchain, federated learning, and knowledge graphs [UT 2016; UT 1426; UT 356; UT 3; UT 56; UT 1890]. Despite these advances, key challenges persist: the lack of data standardization and large-scale, representative datasets; pronounced heterogeneity among food matrices; methodological issues surrounding data imbalance and high dimensionality; and barriers related to interoperability, explainability, security, and regulatory trust [UT 1361; UT 1468; UT 1265; UT 2115; UT 93; UT 1885]. Future research must address the fusion of heterogeneous modalities—combining spectral, sensor, image, and contextual data—alongside advances in adaptive learning, privacy-preserving analytics, large-scale data annotation, and explainable AI for robust, generalizable solutions [UT 1441; UT 524; UT 618; UT 1481; UT 93]. Practically, deploying interoperable, interpretable, and secure analytic infrastructures, validated across diverse food environments, and prioritizing multidisciplinary collaboration among technologists, food scientists, and regulators, will enable standardization, best practice adoption, and user-centric integration [UT 1265; UT 1463; UT 1366; UT 1376; UT 1892; UT 524; UT 356]. Embedding sustainability, transparency, and workforce adaptation into these strategies is essential to supporting the resilience, safety, and quality assurance of modern food systems [UT 56; UT 1400; UT 1890; UT 560; UT 481].
