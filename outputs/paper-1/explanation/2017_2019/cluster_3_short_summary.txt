This cluster in food analytics is defined by the convergence of information technologies, machine learning, semantic frameworks, and domain-specific expertise to acquire, model, and disseminate food and nutrient knowledge across healthcare, agriculture, education, and supply chain contexts [UT 447, UT 474, UT 796, UT 414, UT 1634, UT 457, UT 1720, UT 389, UT 1614, UT 1888; UT 1823, UT 820, UT 835, UT 1686, UT 729; UT 715, UT 1650, UT 619, UT 1668, UT 785, UT 653, UT 1910, UT 1997, UT 1725, UT 2046; UT 448, UT 676, UT 1706, UT 652, UT 507, UT 438]. Anchored by the deployment of ontologies, knowledge graphs, predictive analytics, cloud infrastructures, and IoT-based sensor networks, the current research landscape increasingly emphasizes semantic connectivity, personalization, social and educational interfaces, and system-level interoperability [UT 796, UT 474, UT 729, UT 715, UT 619, UT 835, UT 724, UT 430, UT 676, UT 507, UT 711, UT 1614, UT 1650, UT 1706, UT 653, UT 1668, UT 785, UT 1942, UT 713, UT 648, UT 516, UT 1762]. Major trends include the adoption of deep and multimodal learning for food image recognition, automated nutrient profiling, and fused data-driven insights from textual, visual, and social sources—targeting improvements in food suggestion, health guidance, and sustainability [UT 1614, UT 1720, UT 1706, UT 1650, UT 653, UT 1668, UT 1839, UT 1727, UT 751, UT 641, UT 402]. Despite these advancements, there are persistent challenges: the fragmentation and incomplete linking of food ontologies, integration of heterogeneous and noisy data (from recipes, sensors, social networks), gaps in semantic interoperability, proprietary nutrient sources, and difficulties modeling complex, dynamic food behaviors [UT 796, UT 820, UT 724, UT 430, UT 414, UT 1614, UT 1720, UT 507, UT 676, UT 763, UT 751, UT 1828, UT 1798, UT 1650, UT 648, UT 506, UT 1725, UT 811]. Broader deployment is further hampered by technological silos in IoT ecosystems, inconsistent interface usability, lack of real-world validation, privacy concerns, and uneven digital literacy [UT 449, UT 1888, UT 1997, UT 619, UT 438, UT 507, UT 1706, UT 459, UT 457, UT 444, UT 402, UT 437, UT 434]. Research opportunities thus lie in advancing unified, semantically-rich ontological frameworks; refining multi-modal and context-aware learning; expanding rigorous, user-inclusive evaluation and benchmarking; and promoting transparent, open-access infrastructures that ensure equitable, privacy-compliant personalization at scale [UT 796, UT 474, UT 820, UT 729, UT 619, UT 715, UT 724, UT 430, UT 676, UT 507, UT 423, UT 1928, UT 1577, UT 488, UT 444, UT 474, UT 1888, UT 414, UT 389, UT 1828, UT 1686, UT 713, UT 1891, UT 2046]. It is practically recommended that the field prioritizes efforts to standardize data protocols, accelerate the integration of interoperable knowledge resources, advocate for user-friendly and adaptive interfaces, and foster ongoing interdisciplinary collaboration—including systematic real-world piloting and participatory stakeholder engagement—to maximize the societal and operational impact of intelligent, semantic food analytics systems [UT 751, UT 811, UT 1727, UT 390, UT 447, UT 1798, UT 407, UT 1706, UT 1891, UT 449, UT 713, UT 402, UT 434, UT 785, UT 791, UT 507, UT 590].
