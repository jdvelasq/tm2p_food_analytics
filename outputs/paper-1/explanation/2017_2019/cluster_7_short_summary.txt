The current thematic cluster in food analytics is defined by the integration of intelligent, mobile, and personalized meal recommendation systems, centered on supporting healthier, user-directed, and fast dietary choices across diverse populations, notably including older adults and individuals with specific nutritional needs [UT 389][UT 1650][UT 1798][UT 785][UT 684][UT 648][UT 506][UT 1729][UT 625][UT 776][UT 488][UT 811]. Uniting personalized nutrition, computational intelligence, and mobile health, these systems leverage user and sensor-derived data, such as meal logs, questionnaires, images, and health metrics, to generate adaptive, nutritionist-informed suggestions accounting for calorie requirements, meal timing, preferences, and cultural facets [UT 612][UT 670][UT 662]. Recent trends point towards the widespread adoption of mobile platforms and advanced machine learning for automated food recognition and nutrient analysis—augmenting both real-time convenience and the accuracy of recommendations—while emphasizing user engagement, participatory design, and validation in comparison with nutritionist-guided choices [UT 1650][UT 1798][UT 1729][UT 648][UT 684][UT 389][UT 440][UT 686][UT 662][UT 625][UT 811][UT 776][UT 1828]. Nonetheless, key challenges persist: translating broad dietary guidelines into individualized, relevant interventions remains hampered by heterogeneity in user preferences, device limitations, inconsistent datasets, and integration issues with varied input types or external data sources—yielding barriers to accuracy, engagement, and consistent long-term use [UT 648][UT 776][UT 625][UT 686][UT 440][UT 662][UT 684][UT 1706][UT 1828][UT 1798][UT 407][UT 561][UT 785]. These obstacles are particularly acute among older users and those with chronic conditions, for whom usability, energy efficiency, and adaptability are critical [UT 811][UT 625]. Research opportunities lie in advancing hybrid and ontology-driven recommender approaches, contextual user modeling, large-scale, diverse evaluation datasets, and the development of holistic food indices incorporating satiety, processing levels, and psychological wellbeing [UT 389][UT 407][UT 488][UT 686][UT 684][UT 561][UT 648][UT 776][UT 412][UT 1828][UT 1798][UT 1784][UT 407]. Pushing the field forward, practical enhancement demands systematic validation with real-world data, efficiency-focused app design, adaptive and culturally sensitive interfaces, and flexible, explainable algorithms linked to external health records [UT 684][UT 561][UT 662][UT 440][UT 1650][UT 1798][UT 506][UT 1729][UT 625][UT 811][UT 662][UT 407][UT 389][UT 785][UT 2108][UT 612][UT 670][UT 648][UT 488][UT 625]. Ultimately, sustained collaboration with nutritionists and public health experts is recommended to refine these technologies for maximum practical value—ensuring mobile, user-centric, and personalized food analytics become trusted, effective partners in everyday nutrition and health management [UT 612][UT 469][UT 662][UT 625].
