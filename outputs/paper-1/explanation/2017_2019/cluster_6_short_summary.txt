This cluster, situated at the intersection of nutrition, health, and intelligent computational systems, is fundamentally defined by efforts to foster healthy eating habits, promote prevention, and deliver individualized dietary interventions through innovative technology. Driven by the convergence of artificial intelligence, nutritional science, and user-centric methodologies, emerging trends highlight the widespread adoption of intelligent food recommender systems and advanced analytics that seamlessly integrate diverse streams of personal, behavioral, and contextual data to inform and guide dietary practices across both clinical and everyday settings [UT 838; UT 684; UT 1763; UT 820; UT 713; UT 612; UT 2027; UT 605; UT 625; UT 2001]. A significant methodological evolution is apparent in the use of deep learning, computer vision, and interoperable ontologies, enabling automated food recognition, individualized nutrition tracking, and robust, scalable analytic frameworks for large-scale, real-time dietary monitoring and prevention strategies [UT 414; UT 1566; UT 1839; UT 507; UT 819; UT 1984; UT 387]. Despite these advances, the field continues to grapple with formidable challenges: conceptual hurdles in accurately modeling the dynamic interplay between human nutritional requirements, evolving health states, and complex eating habits; methodological obstacles stemming from heterogeneous, fragmented data and food ontologies; and implementation issues related to creating adaptive, user-friendly prototypes that maintain clinical validity, cultural sensitivity, and engagement, particularly in online and mobile environments and among vulnerable populations [UT 1942; UT 2017; UT 662; UT 785; UT 1763; UT 516; UT 1712]. Addressing these issues opens promising research avenues, such as developing rich, interoperable ontologies, expanding open-access datasets for culturally representative modeling, and advancing context-aware systems that leverage digital twins, knowledge graphs, and multimodal analytics to enable dynamic, life-stage-sensitive and explainable interventions [UT 820; UT 714; UT 407; UT 744; UT 1816; UT 387; UT 670; UT 402; UT 1891]. To bridge computational innovation and practical impact, it is recommended that future research emphasize the direct embedding of expert-validated health guidelines, semantic interoperability, user-centered co-design, privacy, and explainable, adaptive feedback—while aligning system deployment with global public health objectives such as health literacy, prevention, and equity [UT 389; UT 713; UT 605; UT 387; UT 612; UT 2017; UT 611; UT 469; UT 662]. Through these integrated strategies, food analytics can realize its potential to meaningfully bridge the world of intelligent nutrition technologies with real-world eating habits and essential prevention-focused health outcomes for diverse human populations.
