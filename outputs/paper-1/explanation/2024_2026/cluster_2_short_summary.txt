The “deep food analytics” cluster is defined by the integration of advanced artificial intelligence—particularly deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), computer vision, and related data-driven algorithms—with heterogeneous food-related data to automate, scale, and refine food analytics tasks, such as food and ingredient classification, calorie and nutritional estimation, quality and freshness assessment, and dietary management [UT 890][UT 1041][UT 1044][UT 1124][UT 1922][UT 1][UT 166][UT 1155][UT 1145][UT 1008]. Recent trends highlight an accelerated adoption and continual improvement of deep and recurrent architectures, with a shift toward lightweight, edge-adaptable, and multi-modal systems that effectively leverage food images, sensory signals, and nutritional datasets for robust real-world deployment [UT 1041][UT 1044][UT 1922][UT 970][UT 990][UT 894][UT 958][UT 166][UT 248]. The emergence of multi-modal data fusion, transfer learning, federated and generative models, as well as explainable AI, marks a move toward scalable, privacy-aware, and interpretable analytics across broad applications, from dietary recommendation to dynamic supply chain optimization [UT 1074][UT 239][UT 982][UT 893][UT 890][UT 1233][UT 966][UT 974][UT 206][UT 358][UT 1131].
