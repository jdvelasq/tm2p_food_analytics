The current cluster in food analytics is defined by the convergence of artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and deep learning (DL) with the goal of delivering personalized, health-centered, and user-driven dietary guidance through advanced, multi-modal computational frameworks that facilitate precision in food, meal, and restaurant recommendation systems [UT 1189, UT 1041, UT 1122, UT 1231, UT 671, UT 15, UT 45; UT 573, UT 553, UT 225, UT 492, UT 521, UT 527, UT 580, UT 583; UT 585, UT 623, UT 687, UT 739, UT 748, UT 777, UT 814, UT 828, UT 885]. Methodologically, these systems increasingly integrate heterogeneous data—ranging from food images and sensory profiles to user health indicators and omics datasets—to dynamically adapt recommendations to individual dietary, taste, and health needs, underpinned by explainable decision-making and collaborative learning [UT 901, UT 993, UT 894, UT 902, UT 997, UT 1023, UT 1024, UT 1028, UT 1042, UT 1048, UT 1053, UT 1057, UT 1059, UT 1062]. Central trends include the adoption of advanced neural and graph-based architectures, large-scale knowledge fusion, and context-aware personalization to deliver healthy eating and restaurant recommendations with empirical robustness, thereby marking a paradigm shift from preference-driven to health-aware and collaborative analytics [UT 623, UT 527, UT 687, UT 993, UT 1948, UT 792, UT 1048, UT 930, UT 1028, UT 583, UT 1201]. Despite these advances, key challenges persist, including the complexity and diversity of user needs, data heterogeneity and sparsity, algorithmic bias, interpretability limitations, privacy and security issues, and the absence of clinically validated, standardized datasets, all of which hinder accurate modeling, scalable deployment, and user trust [UT 1192, UT 1116, UT 1082, UT 773, UT 974, UT 1231, UT 946, UT 885, UT 1964, UT 1940, UT 777, UT 739, UT 580, UT 583, UT 903, UT 15]. Future research opportunities lie in deploying experimentally validated interventions, deepening integration of multi-omics and real-time health data using privacy-preserving and federated frameworks, enhancing explainable and collaborative recommendation pipelines, engaging users through participatory, gamified, and context-sensitive platforms, and expanding accessibility to global, multilingual contexts [UT 1231, UT 1189, UT 930, UT 221, UT 1099, UT 1029, UT 225, UT 349, UT 332, UT 903]. To maximize practical value, the field should prioritize the advancement of interoperable food ontologies, transparent and interpretable AI, stakeholder co-design, robust privacy mechanisms, and the embedding of health, sustainability, and ethical governance criteria within analytics workflows, further buttressed by real-world validation and educational initiatives to bridge technological innovation with meaningful real-world impact [UT 527, UT 623, UT 691, UT 1062, UT 1966, UT 1972, UT 976, UT 1053, UT 710, UT 1139, UT 1042, UT 792, UT 172, UT 997, UT 1192].
