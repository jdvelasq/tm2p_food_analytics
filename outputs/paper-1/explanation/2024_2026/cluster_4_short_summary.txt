This thematic cluster is defined by the integration of artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing with computational, semantic, and generative approaches applied across the food domain, including recipes, cuisine, flavor, sensory, and agricultural elements. At its conceptual core, the cluster sits at the intersection of multi-modal knowledge—encompassing images, language, sensory, omics, and structured data—to enable comprehensive food analytics and actionable insights [UT 1151; UT 501; UT 2041]. Emerging trends demonstrate a shift from fragmented, unstructured approaches to highly structured, semantically rich models such as food ontologies and knowledge graphs, which underpin improved dietary recommendations, cultural adaptation, and semantic interoperability, driven by large language models and graph-based architectures [UT 573; UT 527; UT 553; UT 687; UT 1041; UT 1; UT 1151; UT 501; UT 814]. Despite progress, persistent challenges include the high heterogeneity, compositional ambiguity, and multicultural complexity in food data, the scarcity of annotated and standardized datasets, unresolved issues in knowledge extraction and semantic interoperability, and the opacity of deep learning models which impact trust, explainability, and regulatory compliance [UT 1; UT 166; UT 225; UT 148; UT 248; UT 527; UT 884; UT 1008; UT 1921; UT 239; UT 573; UT 687; UT 809; UT 1965]. Methodological innovation is urgently needed in the form of robust, domain-adaptive, and explainable multi-modal models, universal ontologies, privacy-preserving architectures such as federated learning, and real-time analytics platforms [UT 1074; UT 671; UT 1233; UT 1008; UT 910; UT 166; UT 224; UT 1122; UT 239; UT 1213]. Future research opportunities include expanding synthetic multi-modal datasets, deploying interpretable generative systems, integrating IoT and blockchain for supply chain traceability, and leveraging social media analytics for real-time trend prediction, all while advancing ethical and cultural considerations [UT 809; UT 1133; UT 1213; UT 206; UT 1016]. To enhance practical value, collaborative development and open sharing of annotated datasets, standardization of semantic models, and embedding explainable, privacy-centric AI into user-facing applications are critical. The alignment of rich food ontologies, late-stage feature fusion, and context-aware natural language processing with real-world deployment—spanning healthcare, supply chain, and consumer contexts—will foster inclusivity, interoperability, and resilience in food analytics solutions, ultimately supporting health, sustainability, and the preservation of culinary diversity [UT 224; UT 248; UT 492; UT 809; UT 997; UT 1179; UT 1231; UT 1156].
